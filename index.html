Safety

Symptoms to Watch

These are the changes that should never be brushed off during stroke recovery.
New facial droop, arm weakness, or sudden speech trouble.
Call now

Treat this as urgent even if it feels similar to the earlier stroke symptoms.

Sudden severe headache, collapse, or unusual confusion.
Emergency

Do not wait to see if it fades. Seek urgent help right away.

Coughing or choking during meals or while taking tablets.
Tell team

Swallow changes can affect safety and may need speech-therapy review.

Home Safety

Safer at Home

A few grounded reminders around meals, walking, and getting help can reduce stress for both you and your caregiver.
Sit fully upright for meals and stay upright after eating.
Meals

This is especially important if swallowing has been slower or coughing has shown up with drinks.

Keep the hallway and bathroom path clear before evening walks.
Walking

Low light and clutter are common triggers for stumbles when fatigue is up.

Leave your medication list and emergency contacts near the bed.
Night

It makes unexpected nighttime concerns a little less chaotic.

Support

Support and Safety

When symptoms change, who to call, how transport works, and the supports that make stroke recovery feel more manageable at home.
Urgent Signs

Get Help Now If

These are the symptom changes that should trigger fast action rather than waiting for the next clinic review.
Your smile changes, speech suddenly worsens, or one side becomes newly weak.
Emergency

Think of these as stroke-return signs until a medical team tells you otherwise.

You cannot swallow safely, choke repeatedly, or feel breathless during meals.
Urgent

Escalate quickly, especially if tablets or fluids are becoming difficult.

A severe new headache, blackout, or collapse appears out of nowhere.
Call now

Do not try to sleep it off or wait until morning.
